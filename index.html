Screensharing (SS / PC check) is how server staff catch client-side cheats that the server can never see. This site teaches the full check — the manual, no-tools way, the automated tools that speed it up safely, and the evidence standard that keeps your bans from being overturned.

The check, start to finish

Every step is standard practice on serious networks. Skip one and the appeal succeeds.

Freeze & brief

Freeze the player in-game and move to a private channel. State exactly why they were checked — never “you look sus”. They keep chat open so they can't claim coercion.

Set the terms

Start recording first. Tell the player their rights: they may record too, a supervisor may sit in, they may refuse — refusal carries a penalty under server rules, not a conviction.

Connect officially

Remote access only through the official tool and the ID your server publishes (AnyDesk / TeamViewer are the norm). The player must be able to verify who you are.

Run the automated scan

Tools like Ocean or FreeEcho scan game files, integrity, and artifacts in seconds. Output is a lead, not a verdict — detections routinely need manual confirmation.

Manual inspection — no tools

Walk the machine yourself: processes and command lines, the .minecraft instance, installed programs and dates, startup entries, prefetch, services. This is where real cases are built — and the skill you practice below.

Verify everything you intend to cite

Open the file. Read the log. Confirm the timestamp. If you can't show it on the recording, it doesn't exist.

Verdict by evidence, then report

Ban only on concrete, rule-aligned evidence — never on reputation, vibes, or a log line you didn't verify. Write the report from the recording.

Without tools: the manual walk

No scanner, no problem. These checks run on a stock Windows machine and cover most of what tools find.

  • 1 · Processes & command linesOpen Task Manager → Details. Read the command line of anything you don't recognize. Is something attached to javaw.exe? Injection leaves a process with a target PID.
  • 2 · The game instance%appdata%\.minecraft → versions, mods, logs. Unknown version folders with their own jars, mods that don't match the server's allowed list, tweaker args in logs\latest.log.
  • 3 · Installed programs & datesSettings → Apps, or Programs & Features. Install dates are sticky evidence: a program installed the day before the flagged clip tells a story.
  • 4 · Startup / Run entriesshell:startup plus the Run keys. Unknown persistence — especially rundll32 pointing at a random file in AppData — rarely belongs to a legit player.
  • 5 · Prefetch (execution history)C:\Windows\Prefetch proves an executable ran, with dates — even after the file is deleted. Requires SysMain to be enabled, which leads to…
  • 6 · Services — the forensic trailCheck EventLog, SysMain, DcomLaunch, DPS, Task Scheduler, DusmSvc. Disabled = deleted evidence = attempted bypass under most servers' rules, and itself a punishable finding.
  • 7 · Reconcile with the scanIf your tool flagged something, confirm it manually. If it flagged nothing, remember: external overlays live outside .minecraft and a clean scan is not a clean machine.

⛔ Stop lines — player rights

Standard SS policy treats these as invasive searches the player may refuse. Cross one and the whole session can be voided — evidence found beside it included:

  • Browser / search historyNot case material. Never open it.
  • Personal files, photos, documentsStay in game, tool, and system-artifact territory.
  • Passwords, tokens, logged-in sessionsReading or capturing credentials is disqualifying.
  • Discord / social dumps and DMsProcess dumps of social apps are off-limits under the usual policy.

The evidence standard

Good checks die bad appeals. Three rules carry most of the weight.

Rule 01

Detection ≠ proof

Automated hits are leads. A hit becomes evidence when you open the artifact on the recording and it says what the tool claimed.

Rule 02

Record everything

The full session, from contact to verdict. No recording means no case: procedural mistakes and missing footage are appeal wins by default.

Rule 03

Presumed innocent

Unclear or controversial evidence goes to the player. A wrong ban costs the server more than a missed one — including your position on staff.

What is a screenshare (SS) on a Minecraft server?

A screenshare — also called a PC check — is a recorded, supervised walkthrough of a player's own computer, run by server staff, to look for client-side cheats the server can never detect. Reach, aim assist, x-ray, wallhacks and injected clients all run on the player's machine, so the server has no way to see them. The staff member connects through the official remote tool, walks the machine by hand, and files a report from what the recording shows.

How do you run a screenshare properly?

Seven steps, in order: freeze the player and move to a private channel; state exactly why they are being checked; start recording before anything else and read out their rights (they may record too, a supervisor may sit in, and they may refuse); connect only through the official remote tool and your server's published ID; run the automated scan if you use one; walk the machine manually; verify by hand every artifact you intend to cite; then file the verdict from the recording. Skipping the recording or the manual verification is how bans get overturned.

What can a player refuse during a Minecraft screenshare?

Standard SS policy treats browser and search history, personal files and photos, passwords, tokens, logged-in sessions, and Discord or social media dumps as invasive searches the player may refuse. A refusal carries a rules penalty — it is not proof of cheating. Opening any of these anyway stamps a procedural breach that can void the whole session and the punishment with it, even if you found something real.

How do you check for cheats manually, with no tools?

Seven checks on a stock Windows machine cover most of what scanners find: processes and command lines (Task Manager → Details, and what is attached to javaw.exe); the game instance in %appdata%\.minecraft; installed programs with their install dates; startup and Run entries; Prefetch execution history in C:\Windows\Prefetch; Windows services (a disabled EventLog, SysMain or DPS is deleted evidence); then reconcile whatever the scan found. This site teaches and drills the whole walk.

What evidence do you need to ban a player for cheating in Minecraft?

Concrete, verified, layered artifacts that you personally opened on the recording — for example a cheat jar whose manifest hashes against nothing Mojang publishes, plus a live process holding a write handle into javaw.exe, plus the launch log proving it loaded. What never meets the bar: a tool detection you never opened, Cheat Engine merely being installed, anticheat flags alone, or a confident accusation in chat. If you cannot show it on the recording, it does not exist.

What are Ocean, FreeEcho and the other Minecraft SS tools, and which are safe?

Ocean (anticheat.ac) is the most widely deployed free automated scanner, with a public freeze plugin. FreeEcho (freeecho.net) is the other long-running platform named in major networks' public SS policies. Both run live investigations on a player's machine, so verify the source yourself before installing anything on staff machines. Microsoft Sysinternals (Process Explorer, Autoruns) is the gold standard and needs no third party at all. See the tool directory and the 7-point safety rubric for the rest, including the scam patterns to avoid.

How do I know if an SS tool is a scam?

Score it on seven points and walk away at three misses: official source only (never a Discord DM, short link, or lookalike domain); verifiable publisher (signed binaries or a public repo); it never asks you to disable antivirus or load unsigned drivers; transparent about what data leaves the machine; honest pricing with no "discounted pins" or cracked builds; used by networks whose public SS policies you can read; and it stays in its lane — tools detect, humans decide. A tool promising "instant bans" or undetectability is selling you someone else's bypass.

Can I practise a Minecraft screenshare before running a real one?

Yes — the practice simulator runs three cases against simulated machines. You are the checker: you run the protocol tests, keep the suspect talking on comms while you work, cite only what you verified by hand, answer a field test, then defend the file when an appeal panel challenges your citations. Case 01 is blatant, case 02 is the deleted-evidence case that looks clean in Task Manager, and case 03 is the one where the correct verdict is to let the player walk. Your report is scored on evidence, procedure and conduct.
