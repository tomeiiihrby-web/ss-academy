Screensharing (SS / PC check) is how server staff catch client-side cheats that the server can never see. This site teaches the full check — the manual, no-tools way, the automated tools that speed it up safely, and the evidence standard that keeps your bans from being overturned.

The check, start to finish

Every step is standard practice on serious networks. Skip one and the appeal succeeds.

Freeze & brief

Freeze the player in-game and move to a private channel. State exactly why they were checked — never “you look sus”. They keep chat open so they can't claim coercion.

Set the terms

Start recording first. Tell the player their rights: they may record too, a supervisor may sit in, they may refuse — refusal carries a penalty under server rules, not a conviction.

Connect officially

Remote access only through the official tool and the ID your server publishes (AnyDesk / TeamViewer are the norm). The player must be able to verify who you are.

Run the automated scan

Tools like Ocean or Echo scan game files, integrity, and artifacts in seconds. Output is a lead, not a verdict — detections routinely need manual confirmation.

Manual inspection — no tools

Walk the machine yourself: processes and command lines, the .minecraft instance, installed programs and dates, startup entries, prefetch, services. This is where real cases are built — and the skill you practice below.

Verify everything you intend to cite

Open the file. Read the log. Confirm the timestamp. If you can't show it on the recording, it doesn't exist.

Verdict by evidence, then report

Ban only on concrete, rule-aligned evidence — never on reputation, vibes, or a log line you didn't verify. Write the report from the recording.

Without tools: the manual walk

No scanner, no problem. These checks run on a stock Windows machine and cover most of what tools find.

  • 1 · Processes & command linesOpen Task Manager → Details. Read the command line of anything you don't recognize. Is something attached to javaw.exe? Injection leaves a process with a target PID.
  • 2 · The game instance%appdata%\.minecraft → versions, mods, logs. Unknown version folders with their own jars, mods that don't match the server's allowed list, tweaker args in logs\latest.log.
  • 3 · Installed programs & datesSettings → Apps, or Programs & Features. Install dates are sticky evidence: a program installed the day before the flagged clip tells a story.
  • 4 · Startup / Run entriesshell:startup plus the Run keys. Unknown persistence — especially rundll32 pointing at a random file in AppData — rarely belongs to a legit player.
  • 5 · Prefetch (execution history)C:\Windows\Prefetch proves an executable ran, with dates — even after the file is deleted. Requires SysMain to be enabled, which leads to…
  • 6 · Services — the forensic trailCheck EventLog, SysMain, DcomLaunch, DPS, Task Scheduler, DusmSvc. Disabled = deleted evidence = attempted bypass under most servers' rules, and itself a punishable finding.
  • 7 · Reconcile with the scanIf your tool flagged something, confirm it manually. If it flagged nothing, remember: external overlays live outside .minecraft and a clean scan is not a clean machine.

⛔ Stop lines — player rights

Standard SS policy treats these as invasive searches the player may refuse. Cross one and the whole session can be voided — evidence found beside it included:

  • Browser / search historyNot case material. Never open it.
  • Personal files, photos, documentsStay in game, tool, and system-artifact territory.
  • Passwords, tokens, logged-in sessionsReading or capturing credentials is disqualifying.
  • Discord / social dumps and DMsProcess dumps of social apps are off-limits under the usual policy.

The evidence standard

Good checks die bad appeals. Three rules carry most of the weight.

Rule 01

Detection ≠ proof

Automated hits are leads. A hit becomes evidence when you open the artifact on the recording and it says what the tool claimed.

Rule 02

Record everything

The full session, from contact to verdict. No recording means no case: procedural mistakes and missing footage are appeal wins by default.

Rule 03

Presumed innocent

Unclear or controversial evidence goes to the player. A wrong ban costs the server more than a missed one — including your position on staff.
